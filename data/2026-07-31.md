### [1] [Same explosion, many faces: numerical modeling reveals viewing angle as a driver of diversity for core-collapse SNe in binary systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28519)
*Soham Mandal,Raphael Baer-way,Shazrene Mohamed,Irene Salmaso,Poonam Chandra,Maryam Modjaz*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: 本文研究了双星系统中稳定洛希瓣溢流导致的核塌缩超新星（CCSNe）。作者用MESA构建了一系列双星演化模型（初始主星16M☉，次星12-15M☉，轨道周期>500天），结合3D星周物质（CSM）结构和Stella辐射流体力学代码计算多波段光变曲线。发现双星驱动的CSM高度非球对称，导致光变曲线强烈依赖观测视角——峰值亮度可相差约5倍，晚期B-V颜色相差约1.5星等。用一维球对称框架解释此类事件会导致爆炸参数偏差达50%、质量损失率偏差超过200%。

Motivation: 大量CCSNe前身星位于双星系统中，双星相互作用会显著影响前身星结构和周围CSM形态，但其对观测到的超新星景观的影响迄今缺乏约束。理解双星如何塑造CSM并影响观测信号，对于正确解释超新星观测数据至关重要。

Method: 1) 使用MESA构建双星演化模型套件（固定初始主星质量16M☉，次星质量12-15M☉，初始轨道周期>500天）；2) 从质量损失历史和轨道动力学生成三维CSM结构；3) 沿三个视线方向提取角度相关的密度剖面；4) 使用辐射流体力学代码Stella计算多波段光变曲线。

Result: 双星驱动的CSM呈现高度非球对称形态，取决于轨道周期和质量比。SN抛射物与这种结构介质的相互作用产生显著的视角依赖效应：峰值亮度因子可达~5，晚期B-V颜色变化~1.5星等。一维球对称假设下推断的爆炸性质偏差达50%，质量损失率偏差超过200%。

Conclusion: 观测到的II型超新星多样性中有相当比例可能源于双星塑造的非对称CSM和观测视角效应，这强烈支持采用多维方法解释此类瞬变事件，以避免系统性的推断偏差。

Abstract: Observable properties of core-collapse supernovae (CCSNe) depend sensitively on the circumstellar material (CSM) formed by pre-explosion mass loss from the progenitor star. Since a large fraction of CCSN progenitors reside in binaries, both the progenitor structure and surrounding CSM can be significantly impacted by binary interaction. Yet, its impact on the observed CCSN landscape remains poorly constrained. In this work, we investigate CCSNe from binary systems undergoing stable Roche lobe overflow. We construct a suite of binary evolution models in \texttt{MESA} with a fixed initial primary mass ($16M_{\odot}$), exploring secondary masses in the range $12-15M_{\odot}$ and initial orbital periods $>500$ days. We generate three-dimensional (3D) CSM structures from the resulting mass-loss histories and orbital dynamics, extract angle-dependent density profiles along three lines of sight, and compute multi-band light curves with the radiation-hydrodynamics code \texttt{Stella}. We find that binary-driven CSM develops highly aspherical morphologies, governed by the orbital period and the mass ratio. Interaction between SN ejecta and this structured medium produces pronounced viewing-angle dependence in the light curves, with peak luminosities differing by factors of $\sim5$ and late-time $B-V$ colors varying by $\sim1.5$ mag depending on observer orientation. We further show that interpreting such events with one-dimensional frameworks assuming isolated progenitors and spherical winds can introduce biases up to $50\%$ for inferred explosion properties and $>200\%$ for inferred mass-loss rates. Our results are consistent with a substantial fraction of interacting Type II SN diversity arising from binary-shaped asymmetric CSM and viewing-angle effects, motivating multidimensional approaches to interpreting these transients.
