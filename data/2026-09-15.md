### [1] [Optical Follow-Up of Two Spider Pulsar Candidates: An Irradiated Redback and a Possible Double Giant Impostor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12618)
*Bettina Due,Jundile Bridziute,Manuel Linares,Marco Turchetta,Maksat Satybaldiev*

Main category: astro-ph.HE

TL;DR: 对两个蜘蛛脉冲星候选体(2FGL J0846.0+2820和4FGL J0935.3+0901)进行光学及多波段跟踪观测，重新确定其本质：J0846并非真正脉冲星候选体，而是分离双红巨星；J0935为处于脉冲星态的redback蜘蛛。

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 紧凑双星毫秒脉冲星("蜘蛛")是不断增长的中子星类别，已知逾100个系统，含30余个未探测到脉冲的候选体。需要多波段跟踪以判定这些候选体的真实性质与当前状态。

Method: 对两个候选体开展光学跟踪观测，并结合红外、X射线和γ射线多波段分析。对J0846分析其轨道光变曲线(2023双峰到2024单峰)及光谱能量分布；对J0935分析光变曲线、颜色与温度变化，并约束伴星基准温度。

Result: J0846光变与辐照或椭球调制均不符，SED显示两个温度、半径相近的黑体成分(光学~6000K/1.8R☉，红外~3400K/5.2R☉)。J0935光变在伴星上合附近出现单一通量极大，颜色与温度升高，显示显著辐照，伴星基准温度约束为4000–4600K。

Conclusion: J0846不是真正的脉冲星候选体，更可能是分离双红巨星系统；J0935符合三种分类中的一种——处于脉冲星态的redback蜘蛛。

Abstract: Compact binary millisecond pulsars (nicknamed "spiders") are a growing class of neutron stars: more than 100 systems are known in the Galactic field, including more than 30 candidates where pulsations have not been detected. Here we report the results of an optical follow-up of two spider pulsar candidates: 2FGL J0846.0+2820 (J0846, a huntsman candidate in an 8.1 d orbit) and 4FGL J0935.3+0901 (J0935, with a 2.4 h orbital period and proposed as a spider of either the transitional, black widow or redback sub-class). We also analyzed their multi-wavelength properties (infrared, X-ray and $γ$-ray bands), in order to assess their nature and current state. The optical orbital light curves of J0846 change from double-peaked in 2023 to single-peaked in 2024, but we argue they are not consistent with either irradiation-driven or ellipsoidal orbital modulation. Its spectral energy distribution shows evidence for two blackbody-like components with similar temperatures ($T$) and radii ($R$), which dominate the optical ($T_2\simeq6000$ K, $R_2\simeq1.8$ R$_\odot$) and infrared ($T_1\simeq3400$ K, $R_1\simeq5.2$ R$_\odot$) bands. Based on this, we argue that J0846 is not a bona fide pulsar candidate and propose an alternative identification: a detached double red giant. In J0935, we find a light curve with one flux maximum around superior conjunction of the companion, where the colors and inferred temperature increase, suggesting prominent irradiation from the pulsar. We place constraints on its companion base temperature ($4000\!-\!4600$ K), which support one of the three classifications proposed for J0935: a redback spider in the pulsar state.
